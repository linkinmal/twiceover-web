The bond market’s best-known recession warning went off in April 2022, and 55 months later no recession has been dated.

## In plain words

The US government borrows for two years and for ten. The rate each loan pays is its yield. The ten-year usually pays more, because lenders want more for tying money up longer. Plot the yields from short loans to long ones and the line slopes up. When the two-year pays more, it slopes down. Traders call that an inverted yield curve.

An inverted curve says bond traders expect short-term rates to fall, and rates fall most often in a recession. So traders have read it as a recession warning for decades. It first inverted in April 2022, for two days, and then stayed inverted from July 2022 for more than two years. Before each of the six US recessions since 1976, the recession arrived within three years of the curve inverting. This time the economy kept growing.

## 1. The facts

- **The signal:** the 10-year Treasury yield closes below the 2-year.
- **This time:** below on two days in April 2022. Then below every day the bond market was open from 6 July 2022 to 26 August 2024, and twice more that September.
- **The record:** six recessions since 1976, each after an inversion.
- **Since April 2022:** no recession dated, and output up every quarter through September 2026.

## 2. Every wait since 1976

The National Bureau of Economic Research, the NBER, dates US recessions. It names the month the economy peaked, and the recession starts the month after. The table counts from the first close below zero to that peak. The spread is the 10-year yield minus the 2-year, from the Federal Reserve’s daily series, which starts in June 1976. Below zero means the 2-year pays more.

| The economy peaked | Curve first closed below zero | Months between |
|---|---|---|
| January 1980 | August 1978 | 17 |
| July 1981 | September 1980 | 10 |
| July 1990 | December 1988 | 19 |
| March 2001 | February 2000 | 13 |
| December 2007 | December 2005 | 24 |
| February 2020 | August 2019 | 6 |
| No peak dated | April 2022 | 55 by November 2026 |

The 1981 row counts from September 1980, when the curve turned below zero again after the 1980 recession. The curve also dipped below zero on and off from late May to late July 1998. Counted from that dip, the 2001 wait grows to 34 months. The current wait is 55 months.

## 3. The clock from the end of the inversion ran out too

A second reading of the signal watches the end of the inversion. In four of the six cycles since 1976 the curve turned positive before the economy peaked. In each of those four, the peak came within six months.

- **1980 and 1981:** the economy peaked while the curve was still inverted.
- **1990:** four months from the last close below zero, in March, to the July peak.
- **2001:** three months, from December 2000 to March 2001.
- **2007:** six months, from June to December.
- **2020:** six months, from August 2019 to February 2020.
- **Now:** 26 months since the last close below zero, on 5 September 2024.

## 4. What the economy did instead

Real output, gross domestic product after inflation, grew in every quarter from the second quarter of 2022 through the third quarter of 2026, in today’s revised figures. From the second quarter of 2022 to the third quarter of 2026 it rose 12.0%.

Payrolls rose from 153.1 million jobs in July 2022 to 159.0 million in September 2026. Unemployment climbed from 3.5% to 4.2%. That is a slower job market. The NBER has dated no recession since the one that ended in April 2020.

## 5. Two details the record leaves out

The 2022 inversion was the longest in the series. It ran 537 days of bond trading without a break. The next longest, from 1978 to 1980, ran 423. The 2022 inversion’s deepest point, 1.08 percentage points below zero on 3 July 2023, was the deepest since 1981.

The 2020 match is thin. In 2019 the curve closed below zero on three trading days in late August. The recession that followed came from a pandemic. The signal’s record of six from six counts it anyway.

## 6. The verdict

The signal earned its fame by matching all six recessions since 1976. In 2022 the signal ran longer than any inversion since 1976, and deeper than any since 1981. Output then grew through all of it and for two years after it. On this cycle, the yield curve’s recession call was wrong.

## 7. Where the curve stands now

On 30 October 2026 the 10-year paid 0.38 percentage points more than the 2-year. The 10-year has not closed below the 2-year since 5 September 2024.
