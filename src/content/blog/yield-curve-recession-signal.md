The bond market’s best-known recession warning went off in July 2022, and 52 months later no recession has come.

## In plain words

The US government borrows for two years and for ten. The ten-year loan usually pays the higher rate, because lenders want more for tying money up longer. When the two-year pays more, the line through those rates slopes down. Traders call that an inverted yield curve.

Traders have read an inverted curve as a recession warning for decades. It inverted in July 2022 and stayed that way for more than two years. Before each of the six US recessions since 1976, the recession arrived within three years of the curve inverting. This time the economy kept growing.

## 1. The facts

- **The signal:** the 10-year Treasury yield closes below the 2-year.
- **This time:** below on every trading day from 6 July 2022 to 26 August 2024, and on two more days in September 2024.
- **The record:** six recessions since 1976, each after an inversion.
- **Since July 2022:** no recession dated, and output up every quarter.

## 2. Every wait since 1976

The National Bureau of Economic Research, the NBER, dates US recessions. It names the month the economy peaked, and the recession starts the month after. The table counts from the first close below zero to that peak. The spread is the Federal Reserve’s daily series, which starts in June 1976.

| The economy peaked | Curve first closed below zero | Months between |
|---|---|---|
| January 1980 | August 1978 | 17 |
| July 1981 | September 1980 | 10 |
| July 1990 | December 1988 | 19 |
| March 2001 | February 2000 | 13 |
| December 2007 | December 2005 | 24 |
| February 2020 | August 2019 | 6 |
| No peak dated | July 2022 | 52 by November 2026 |

The curve also dipped below zero for a few weeks in mid-1998. Counted from that dip, the 2001 wait grows to 34 months. The current wait is 52 months and still running.

## 3. The second clock ran out too

A second reading of the signal watches the end of the inversion. In past cycles the recession often began soon after the curve turned positive again. That clock has run out as well.

- **1980 and 1981:** the recession began while the curve was still inverted.
- **1990:** four months from the last close below zero, in March, to the July peak.
- **2001:** three months, from December 2000 to March 2001.
- **2007:** six months, from June to December.
- **2020:** six months, from August 2019 to February 2020.
- **Now:** 26 months since the last close below zero, on 5 September 2024.

## 4. What the economy did instead

Real output, gross domestic product after inflation, grew in every quarter from the second quarter of 2022 through the third quarter of 2026. From the third quarter of 2022 to the third quarter of 2026 it rose 11.2%.

Payrolls rose from 153.1 million jobs in July 2022 to 159.0 million in September 2026. Unemployment rose too, from 3.5% to 4.2%. That is a slower job market. The NBER has dated no recession since the one that ended in April 2020.

## 5. Two details the record leaves out

The 2022 inversion was the longest in the series. It ran 537 trading days without a break. The next longest, from 1978 to 1980, ran 423. Its deepest point, 1.08 percentage points below zero on 3 July 2023, was the deepest since 1981.

The 2020 hit is thin. In 2019 the curve closed below zero on three trading days in late August. The recession that followed came from a pandemic. The signal’s record of six from six counts it anyway.

## 6. The verdict

An inverted curve says bond traders expect short-term rates to fall. Rates fall most often in a recession, which is why the signal earned its fame. In 2022 the signal ran longer and deeper than ever before. The economy then grew through all of it and two years past it. On this cycle, the yield curve’s recession call was wrong.

## 7. Where the curve stands now

On 30 October 2026 the 10-year paid 0.38 percentage points more than the 2-year. The 10-year has not closed below the 2-year since 5 September 2024.
