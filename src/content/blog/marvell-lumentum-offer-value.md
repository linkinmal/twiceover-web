Marvell's offer for Lumentum is paid in Marvell shares, so its dollar value moves every day Marvell trades. Each Lumentum share becomes 4.5 Marvell shares when the deal closes. The joint release priced that at "approximately $1,132", using Marvell's close on 28 October. At Monday's close, Marvell stood at $171.41 and the same 4.5 shares were worth $771.35.

## 1. What the filing says

Lumentum filed the agreement on Monday morning in a Form 8-K, the report a US company files with the SEC when something material happens. Five lines of it carry the deal.

| From the filing and the release | |
|---|---|
| What one Lumentum share becomes | 4.500 Marvell shares, a "fixed exchange ratio" |
| Dollar value in the release | About $1,132, on 28 October closing prices |
| Premium in the release | About 23.9%, on the same closing prices |
| Expected closing | The first half of 2028 |
| Date after which either side may walk away | 1 November 2027, which can stretch to 1 August 2028 while regulators decide |

The release picks 28 October because it was "the last trading day before market speculation regarding a potential transaction began." The agreement has no collar, the clause some stock deals add to top up the shares when the buyer's price falls. So the 4.5 never changes, and the dollars follow Marvell.

## 2. The same 4.5 shares at three closes

| Nasdaq close | Marvell | 4.5 Marvell shares | Lumentum | Lumentum against the 4.5 shares |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wed 28 Oct, before deal talk | $251.65 | $1,132.43 | $913.95 | $218.48 below |
| Fri 30 Oct, last close before signing | $196.40 | $883.80 | $840.70 | $43.10 below, 4.9% |
| Mon 2 Nov, announcement day | $171.41 | $771.35 | $684.40 | $86.95 below, 11.3% |

Marvell fell 22% in the two sessions after the talk began. The two companies signed on Sunday 1 November. At Friday's close, the 4.5 shares were already worth $883.80. That is $30.15 below Lumentum's own close on 28 October, so the deal was signed at a value under Lumentum's price before anyone knew of it.

On Monday, Marvell fell another 12.7% and Lumentum fell 18.6%. Lumentum also reported its own quarter that morning. By the close, the offer was worth 31.9% less than the figure in the release.

## 3. What the $86.95 pays for

Lumentum's price follows Marvell's because the payment is Marvell stock. Our post on [Silence Therapeutics and Merck KGaA's $20 cash offer](/blog/silence-therapeutics-merck-spread) works through a cash deal, where the gap to the offer was 42 cents.

Funds that trade announced deals do it in two legs. They buy one Lumentum share and sell short 4.5 Marvell shares, which means borrowing those shares and selling them now. If the deal closes, the Lumentum share turns into exactly the 4.5 Marvell shares they owe. Marvell's price then stops mattering to them, and the gap is what they keep.

At Monday's close that gap was $86.95 on a $684.40 share. That is 12.7% before borrowing costs and dividends. The release expects the wait to run into the first half of 2028.

## 4. What has to happen first

The 8-K lists the conditions. These four carry the risk:

1. Lumentum's shareholders adopt the agreement.
2. Marvell's shareholders approve issuing the new shares.
3. The US antitrust waiting period runs out, and "certain other required regulatory approvals" arrive.
4. No court or government order blocks the merger.

The agreement names those other approvals in a schedule to a disclosure letter, and the letter is not public.

The filing also prices a failure. If the deal dies because regulators block it or the last outside date passes, Marvell pays Lumentum $3.2 billion. If Marvell's board walks away from its recommendation, it pays $5.8 billion. Lumentum owes $2.9 billion in the matching cases.

## 5. What the price says now

Until the deal closes or breaks, a Lumentum share trades as 4.5 Marvell shares less the gap. A $1 move in Marvell is worth about $4.50 on a Lumentum share. The gap more than doubled on announcement day, from 4.9% to 11.3%. The agreement gives regulators until August 2028, and Monday's price says the market expects them to use a large part of it.
