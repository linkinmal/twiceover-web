Index funds now move so much money on Russell’s rebalance day that it was the busiest trading day of 2026 by dollars. The rebalance resets which companies belong in Russell’s US stock indexes. On 11 December it runs in December for the first time in more than three decades. The funds will trade at that Friday’s closing price.

## The facts

- **The indexes:** the Russell 1000 holds large US companies and the Russell 2000 small ones. The Russell 3000 holds both.
- **The rebalance:** FTSE Russell, the company behind them, resets which companies belong in each. It calls this the reconstitution.
- **The day:** funds that copy an index trade into the new list at the closing price on the day it takes effect.
- **June 2026:** $2,002 billion traded on Friday 26 June, against $1,006 billion on the median day of 2026 so far. No day in Cboe’s files back to 2009 traded more.

## 1. Why one closing price draws so much money

An index fund holds what its index holds. When a company joins the Russell 2000, every fund that copies the index has to own it once the new list takes effect. That moment is the close of the rebalance day. A fund that buys at that closing price pays the same price its index uses. A fund that buys a day early carries the risk that the price moves overnight. So the funds wait, and they all trade at the same close. Companies leaving the index are sold the same way, at the same price. FTSE Russell counts $553.9 billion traded at that close on 26 June, in the closing auctions of the NYSE and Nasdaq alone. Set against our count of the whole day on every venue, that is more than a quarter of it, in one auction.

## 3. 2016, when the UK’s vote landed on the same Friday

The UK voted on leaving the European Union on Thursday 23 June 2016. The result came overnight, before US trading opened on Friday 24 June. That Friday was also the Russell rebalance day. It traded $574 billion, 2.21 times a normal day of 2016. Two causes met in one session, so the 2016 figure does not measure the rebalance alone. Leave 2016 out, and this June’s 1.99 times is the highest in the record.

## 4. What 11 December changes

From 1989 to 2025 the rebalance came once a year, in June. From 2026 FTSE Russell runs it twice, in June and in December. The December lists take effect after the close on Friday 11 December. The funds trade at that day’s closing price and hold the new lists when trading opens on Monday 14 December. Stock and index options and quarterly index futures expire one week later, on Friday 18 December. [Our post on an expiring option’s last 90 minutes](/blog/expiring-option-after-the-close) covers what can still happen to an option after that close.

We expect 11 December to rank among the ten busiest days of 2026 by dollars traded. The tenth busiest so far traded $1,395 billion. The June rebalance day ranked in the top ten of its own year in 7 of the 18 years in the table, and in each of the last three. The funds have to trade at that close however short the list of changes is.
