Home Depot raised what it expects to sell this year and narrowed what it expects to earn per share to the middle of its old range. Its margin forecast slipped to the bottom. That is the plain reading of the third-quarter report it put out before Tuesday's open, on the same morning it named a new chief executive. The picture above shows what options priced for its last eight reports, read in section 5.

## 1. The facts

| The quarter to 1 November | |
|---|---|
| Sales | $43.4 billion, up 4.9% on a year ago |
| Gross profit | Up 2.9% |
| Operating income | Up 2.4% |
| Earnings per share, unadjusted | $3.72, up from $3.62 |

Sales grew twice as fast as operating income. Gross margin, the share of sales left after the cost of the goods, fell from 33.4% to 32.8% on the release's own figures.

## 2. August against November

The release says it "updates" the guidance for the year. Set beside the August release, each range became a single figure. Adjusted figures leave out the yearly charge for the intangible value of businesses Home Depot bought.

| Fiscal 2026 | August | November |
|---|---|---|
| Sales growth | 2.5% to 4.5% | About 4.5% |
| Operating margin | 12.4% to 12.6% | About 12.4% |
| Adjusted earnings per share growth | Flat to 4.0% | About 2.0% |
| Earnings per share growth | Flat to 4.0% | About 1.5% |

Sales growth now sits at the top of the August range. Operating margin is what is left of each sales dollar after the goods, the staff, the stores and the other running costs are paid for. Its new figure is the bottom of the August range. Against that range's 12.5% middle, Home Depot now keeps 10 cents less of every $100 it sells. Against the middle of the sales range, it expects one point more growth. The two come out almost even for operating income. Adjusted earnings per share land on 2.0%, the exact middle of August's range. The unadjusted figure lands half a point below that middle.

## 3. Where the sales came from

Comparable sales leave out stores and businesses too new to compare with last year. They rose 2.2%. Comparable customer transactions fell 0.5%, and the average ticket rose 3.0% to $93.09. Each sale was bigger, and there were slightly fewer of them.

Total sales grew faster, 4.9%, because they also count what the comparable figure leaves out. The visit and ticket figures leave out SRS and HD Supply, the company's suppliers to contractors, so they do not multiply out to the 2.2%.

## 4. The new chief executive

Richard McPhail becomes chief executive on 23 November. He has been finance chief since 2019. The company's release credits him with overseeing the purchases of HD Supply, SRS and GMS, suppliers to contractors. Ted Decker, back from a medical leave, steps down and stays as chair. The quarter's release already quotes McPhail as incoming chief executive: "Results in the third quarter exceeded our expectations."

He takes over a forecast that grows sales more than twice as fast as earnings per share.

## 5. What the options priced

At Monday's close, the at-the-money straddle for Friday's expiry cost 4.8% of Home Depot's share price. A straddle is a call and a put at the same strike, so it pays when the stock moves far in either direction. That 4.8% is the size of move the options market priced for the report, through Friday.

The picture above shows the last eight reports from TwiceOver's Earnings history section. Each box is the move the options priced, which the section calls the implied move. Each dot is the move from the close before a report to the close after it. The last box is Tuesday's report, priced at Monday's close. In seven reports with a result, the stock moved more than priced once: in November 2025 it fell 6.0% against 4.2%. The average move, up or down, was 2.2%, about half the 4.3% the options priced on average. Our post on [25 stocks and the move options priced for earnings](/blog/earnings-implied-move-average) shows how often that holds across other companies. When the move comes in under the price, a call bought the day before can lose money on a rise, as [our IV crush example](/blog/iv-crush-call-before-earnings) works out.
