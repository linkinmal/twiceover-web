Nvidia’s board put Jensen Huang back in charge on Sunday, 11 days after he resigned. The release that announced it also says the board’s own investigation “will proceed to conclusion.” So he returns as chief executive and director while that work is still running. The board gives no date for its end.

## 1. What the 12 November release said

The board accepted Huang’s resignation as president, chief executive and director on 11 November, “effective immediately.” It announced it the next morning and gave no reason. Colette Kress, the finance chief since 2013, became interim chief executive and kept the finance job. Harvey Jones, a director since 1993, became what the release calls the board’s “first-ever” chair. The board said it would search for a permanent chief executive among people inside and outside the company.

## 2. What the 23 November release says

The vote to bring him back was unanimous. It followed “a series of discussions and meetings” with Huang, and the release gives the board’s reason in one line:

> The board “was satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead NVIDIA.”

The board also took input from “senior levels of the United States government.” Based on that input, the board says it is confident his return “poses no national security risk.”

Then comes the paragraph that keeps the matter open. It refers to “recent reporting that Mr. Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort,” meaning a foreign spy service went after him. In response, the board set up a committee of independent directors to investigate, with independent lawyers. The new release says that investigation goes on to its end. Huang “fully supports the investigation,” in the release’s words.

The release lists three steps the 12 November one did not mention. The board has formed a National Security and Export Control Committee. Nvidia is searching for a chief compliance officer, a new post. And the company “will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.”

Kress remains finance chief, and Jones remains chair of the board.

## 4. The quarterly report had not come by Tuesday

Nvidia put out its third-quarter results on 17 November. The full quarterly report, called the 10-Q, carries the notes to the accounts, the risks and the legal matters. Nvidia filed its last nine quarterly and annual reports on the same day as its results. Since November 2020 no 10-Q came more than seven days after the results.

Early on Tuesday 24 November, the SEC’s filing list for Nvidia showed no 10-Q for this quarter. That is seven days, level with the longest wait in that stretch. The quarter ended on 25 October. A company of Nvidia’s size has 40 days from there, which runs to Friday 4 December.

The results release carries two new lines. Its list of forward-looking statements, the matters it says could turn out differently, now includes “expectations with respect to NVIDIA’s Form 10-Q for the third quarter” and “the ongoing investigation by an independent committee.” August’s release had no such lines.

## 5. Our projection

The picture above is TwiceOver’s analysis of Nvidia, run on Monday at 10:56 a.m. New York with the stock at $167.76. Our projection points to $178 around 8 December, $192 around 25 February and $205 around 26 May. The far price sits near the stock’s 50-day and 200-day averages, about $207 and $203 that morning.

Two things are still open. The 10-Q is due by 4 December. The committee’s investigation has no end date, and the board has not said it will publish a finding.

The two leadership announcements reached the SEC as current reports, the form a company files within four business days of a major event.
